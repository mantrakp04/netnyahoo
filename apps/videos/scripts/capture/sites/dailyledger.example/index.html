The quiet return of the paper map

Hikers, cyclists and the newly lost are folding their routes again. A small press in the hills can't print them fast enough.

By Ana Ortiz · 6 min read

On a Saturday morning in late September, the shop at the end of the high street has a queue. Not for coffee: for maps, the folding kind, printed on paper that survives a downpour and a dropped thermos.

"People come in with a phone at four per cent," says the owner, who has run the press for thirty years. "They leave with something that doesn't need charging."
