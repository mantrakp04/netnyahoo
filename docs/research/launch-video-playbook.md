# Launch video playbook

A working playbook for a 20-35 s launch video on X (4:5, autoplays muted, loops). Built from web research in September 2026 plus my own judgment, which is labelled as such. Sources are linked inline and listed at the end.

A caveat on sources: the makers we most admire (The Browser Company, Linear, Raycast, Apple) have published almost nothing about how they cut their launch films. What exists is secondhand analysis. Where a rule rests on that analysis or on my own reading of the genre, I say so.

## 1. The hook in the first second

1. **Frame 0 has to be moving, and it has to be the idea.** X counts attention in the first two or three seconds, and those seconds play muted ([Blitzcut, X captions guide](https://blitzcutai.com/blog/how-to-add-captions-x-twitter-video)). Publishers who grew up on muted Facebook autoplay learned the same thing: catch the eye right away, because a view counts at 3 s ([Digiday, "85 percent of Facebook video is watched without sound"](https://digiday.com/media/silent-world-facebook-video/)).
2. **No logo sting, no "Introducing", no greeting.** Open on the product or the problem ([Okara, viral launch on X handbook](https://okara.ai/viral-launch-x-handbook)). The logo belongs at the end, where it doubles as the loop seam.
3. **Put the hook line on screen within 0.5 s, under 10 words, and make it the largest text in the piece** ([RocketShip HQ, text overlays in video ads](https://www.rocketshiphq.com/text-overlays-video-ads-mobile/)).
4. **The post text is part of the hook.** People read the tweet before deciding to watch, so the copy above the video and the first frame should set each other up, not repeat each other (search results summarising X video guidance; my judgment on the pairing).

## 2. Sound-off legibility

5. **Assume nobody hears it.** Most X timeline impressions are muted autoplay with captions off, so an uploaded SRT does nothing in the feed. Burn the words in ([Blitzcut](https://blitzcutai.com/blog/how-to-add-captions-x-twitter-video)). The old industry figure is 85-90% silent views for branded video on Facebook ([Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/silent-world-facebook-video/)).
6. **The silent cut has to work as a complete film. Sound then adds feeling on top.** Test by watching it muted on a phone at arm's length. If a joke only lands with the VO, it needs a text version too.
7. **Caption spec:** each line 6-8% of frame height, heavy sans, at most 2 lines, kept clear of the bottom edge where the progress bar sits ([Blitzcut](https://blitzcutai.com/blog/how-to-add-captions-x-twitter-video)). On a 1080x1350 frame that means roughly 80-110 px cap-to-descender per line.
8. **Reading time:** at least 1.5 s per line and 2.5 s for a 10-word card. Use 5-8 words per card. Put text on screen 0.1-0.3 s before the matching VO word ([RocketShip HQ](https://www.rocketshiphq.com/text-overlays-video-ads-mobile/)).
9. **Burned-in text and VO should not be verbatim transcripts of each other in a satire piece** (my judgment). Use captions for the VO lines that carry the joke. Use on-screen graphics (lower thirds, the disclaimer, a stamped word) for the rest. A full transcript in karaoke style reads as a TikTok explainer, not as a campaign ad.

## 3. Cut rhythm and pacing

10. **Know the baseline.** One study of cinematic social ads found an average length of 17.9 s, 15.2 shots and 1.2 s per shot, against roughly 4 s per shot in modern film ([Law et al., "How Do Professional Editors Evaluate the Editing Quality of AI-Generated Cinematic Video Ads?", arXiv 2608.24329](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24329), figures as surfaced in search results). Older research on TV spots makes the same tradeoff: shots held too long bore people, and shots cut too soon lose the message ([Gale, "Camera shot length in TV commercials"](https://link.gale.com/apps/doc/A13980912/AONE?u=googlescholar&sid=AONE&xid=bd8a2091), search result).
11. **Aim for a varied rhythm, not a uniform one.** For a 25 s piece: 12-18 shots. Fast runs of 0.5-1.0 s shots, then 2-3 holds of 2.5-3.5 s on the shots that carry the idea (my judgment, anchored to rule 10). Last time the problem was one shot held for 20 s. Chopping everything to 1.2 s would be the opposite mistake.
12. **Lay the music first and cut picture to it** ([PremiumBeat, "Cutting Commercials"](https://www.premiumbeat.com/blog/cutting-commercials-editing-15-30-and-60-second-spots/)). Don't cut on every beat, because that feels frantic and predictable. Put major cuts on downbeats and break the pattern on purpose now and then ([LBBOnline, "More than Just Cutting to the Beat"](https://lbbonline.com/news/more-than-just-cutting-to-the-beat-the-nuance-of-editing-music-videos), search result summary). Sound designers warn about the same thing: cutting on too many beats pulls attention off the picture ([Pitchdrift, sound design tips](https://pitchdrift-productions.com/sound-design-tips-for-motion-graphics/)).
13. **Use speed ramps inside one shot.** Run the dull parts of a UI take at 1.5-2x ([Flowjam, startup demo video guide](https://www.flowjam.com/blog/startup-product-demo-video); [Tella, speed ramp](https://www.tella.com/definition/speed-ramp), search result). Ease back to 1x right before the proof moment. One analysis says Linear-style videos rush the easy steps and hit the brakes on the step that shows the product's real mechanism ([Advids, SaaS UI video examples](https://advids.co/blog/saas-ui-video)).
14. **Get to the payoff early.** When a Geico spot is cut from 60 s to 30 s, the payoff moves earlier, not later ([PremiumBeat](https://www.premiumbeat.com/blog/cutting-commercials-editing-15-30-and-60-second-spots/)). Getting shorter means deleting good footage.

## 4. Typography in motion

15. **Simple entrances only:** fade, pop or scale-up, 150-250 ms. No letter-by-letter builds, no 3D type, no flying words. Elaborate kinetic type slows comprehension ([RocketShip HQ](https://www.rocketshiphq.com/text-overlays-video-ads-mobile/)).
16. **Keep one type system for the whole piece.** Here that means the genre's own system: a condensed serif or slab for the campaign lower third, a plain sans for captions. (My judgment. The campaign-ad look comes from type and color more than from motion.)
17. **Text needs contrast:** 4.5:1 minimum, with a 3-4 px stroke or a 60-80% plate behind text over footage ([RocketShip HQ](https://www.rocketshiphq.com/text-overlays-video-ads-mobile/)).
18. **Keep text inside a safe frame of about 60 px on the sides and clear of the bottom ~15%** (adapted from RocketShip's per-platform margins; X publishes none, so this is my judgment).

## 5. Showing UI

19. **Use the real product, recorded for real.** Screen recordings of the product working beat any claim a VO can make ([Speedrun/a16z, "How to Make a Viral Launch Video"](https://speedrun.substack.com/p/how-to-make-a-viral-launch-video); [Cursor launch analysis, dev.to](https://dev.to/fmerian/the-cursor-way-to-launch-5e1m): product screenshots only, no stock). Use real data, not a fake test account ([Flowjam](https://www.flowjam.com/blog/startup-product-demo-video)).
20. **Never show a full window at thumbnail scale.** A 1440 px-wide window squeezed into a 4:5 phone frame makes every label about 4 px tall. Crop to the one detail that proves the claim (the ad counter, the sidebar row, the address bar) and hold on one meaningful product state ([Screenhance, animated app demos 2026](https://screenhance.com/blog/animated-app-demos-2026)). My rule: if the key UI text isn't at least 3% of frame height, crop tighter.
21. **Direct the cursor like an actor.** Click-driven zooms that push in, hold a beat and pull out, plus smoothed cursor paths, are why Screen Studio demos look finished ([Screen Studio](https://screen.studio/), search summary). Linear-style videos move the cursor on fast, smooth curves to pull the eye to the next thing ([Advids](https://advids.co/blog/saas-ui-video)). Enlarge the cursor. Hide it when it isn't doing anything ([Flowjam](https://www.flowjam.com/blog/startup-product-demo-video)).
22. **No 3D device spins.** When the viewer has to read the screen, the screen should stay flat and still, and camera motion should be a slow push-in or a slide. (My judgment. Mockup vendors themselves say to use rotation only when it serves the story ([Screenhance](https://screenhance.com/blog/animated-app-demos-2026)).)
23. **Apple's polish comes from lighting and grading, not the camera** ([Prolost, "What Does and Doesn't Matter about Apple Shooting their October Event on iPhone"](https://prolost.com/blog/scarybts)). For UI footage the equivalent is a clean capture at 2x, a color treatment that matches the grade, and a consistent wallpaper and window chrome in every shot (my judgment).

## 6. Music

24. **Music sets the emotion more than the words do.** Ted Brader showed that swapping only the music and imagery under the same script and narration changes what viewers feel. Tense music with grainy footage produces fear. Warm images with an uplifting score produce enthusiasm ([CBS News, "The Psychology of Political Ads"](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/the-psychology-of-political-ads/)).
25. **Buy or commission music that sounds played.** Music supervisors prize tracks that sound genuinely performed, whether that's real players or very expressive samples. Libraries now stock band-made music for exactly that reason ([search results incl. Sound On Sound, "All About Library Music"](https://www.soundonsound.com/music-business/all-about-library-music-part-4)). The last cut sounded synthetic. Quantized MIDI strings with no bow noise are the tell (my judgment).
26. **Cut the track to length. Don't fade it out.** Pick a piece with a clear downbeat structure, edit it to the picture so it resolves on the final card, and make the last bar lead back into the first for the loop (my judgment; PremiumBeat notes libraries supply 15/30/60 s cutdowns).

## 7. SFX: UI foley, not whooshes

27. **If every animation gets a whoosh, the audio turns into noise. Good sound design knows when to stay quiet** ([Motion the Agency, sound design before/after](https://www.motiontheagency.com/blog/sound-design-examples-before-after-in-motion)). Stock sounds reused enough times become recognisable clichés ([Wikipedia, "Stock sound effect"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stock_sound_effect), search result).
28. **Use foley for real actions:** a key tap, a trackpad click, the soft thunk of a tab closing, landing on the frame where the visual action completes ([Motion the Agency](https://www.motiontheagency.com/blog/sound-design-examples-before-after-in-motion)). Record your own keyboard and trackpad. They cost nothing and nobody else has them ([Pitchdrift](https://pitchdrift-productions.com/sound-design-tips-for-motion-graphics/) on custom foley).
29. **Use silence as a cut.** Dropping the music for 0.5-1 s before the punchline is the cheapest way to make a line land (my judgment; Pitchdrift and Motion the Agency both treat silence as a tool).
30. **Loudness:** X publishes no target. The consensus safe setting is -14 LUFS integrated with a -1 dBTP limiter ([Dan Murtagh, LUFS standards](https://danmurtagh.com/lufs-loudness-standards); [Critical Listening Lab](https://www.criticallisteninglab.com/en/learn/loudness/social-media)). Compress the VO harder than you would for a podcast (4:1 to 6:1) and check the mix on a phone speaker at medium volume ([Critical Listening Lab](https://www.criticallisteninglab.com/en/learn/loudness/social-media)).

## 8. VO or no VO, and how to avoid the stock-TTS feel

31. **A demo can run with no VO.** The 23 s Claude x Blender demo had none and did fine ([Speedrun/a16z](https://speedrun.substack.com/p/how-to-make-a-viral-launch-video)). Parody is different: the genre voice is the joke. A campaign ad without its narrator is just a montage.
32. **Word budget:** conversational VO runs about 2.5 words/s, so ~75 words fills 30 s ([Blue Wave Voiceover](https://www.bluewavevoiceover.com/the-number-of-words-in-political-ad-scripts/); [Epolitics](https://www.epolitics.com/2020/05/19/eight-best-practices-for-effective-political-voiceover/)). Emotional reads take longer because of pauses and stretched vowels. For deadpan in 25 s, write 35-50 words (my judgment). Every word you cut buys a pause.
33. **AI voices mostly sound fake because of timing, not tone.** Pauses run too long, cadence stays uniform, and generated chunks leave gaps. Cut pauses over 1-2 s and keep 200-500 ms breaths ([SilentCut, "Why AI Voices Sound Unnatural"](https://silentcut.studio/blog/why-ai-voices-sound-unnatural)). Write the pauses in explicitly with break tags and delivery tags ([ElevenLabs, less robotic TTS](https://elevenlabs.io/blog/how-to-make-text-to-speech-sound-less-robotic)).
34. **Direct the read like a session.** Give the talent or model a reference ad for tone and pace ([Epolitics](https://www.epolitics.com/2020/05/19/eight-best-practices-for-effective-political-voiceover/)). Generate each line separately, 10-20 takes per line, and comp the best ones. Hand-place every pause on the timeline to the picture. Put room tone under the VO so it doesn't float in digital silence (my judgment). One read straight through a script is exactly what "stock TTS" sounds like.
35. **Cast the register, not a celebrity.** The political-ad reference is Hal Riney, whose voice was described as "the pack of Marlboros and the bourbon voice" ([Retro Report transcript](https://retroreport.org/transcript/transcript-morning-in-america-political-ads-that-changed-the-game/)). Warm, low, unhurried, sure of itself ([Wikipedia, "Bear in the woods"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bear_in_the_woods)). Voice mismatch with the visuals is the fastest way to make an AI voice sound fake (search results on AI voiceover).

## 9. Length and the loop

36. **X auto-loops videos of 60 s or less** ([Digital Information World, Twitter FAQ change](https://www.digitalinformationworld.com/2019/02/under-60-seconds-twitter-videos-loop.html); the policy dates from 2019, so check it on a real post). A 25 s piece will be seen two or three times by anyone who stops scrolling.
37. **Design the seam.** The last frame, the motion and the sound should all lead back into the first frame, so the restart isn't noticed ([Vibbit loop guide](https://vibbit.ai/blog/seamless-video-loops-guide), search result). The end card can double as a pre-roll for frame 0.
38. **Length:** 20-30 s for this piece. Feature clips run 15-45 s; hero launch films average about 70 s ([Motion the Agency, launch video guide](https://www.motiontheagency.com/blog/product-launch-video-guide)). A loop-native X piece should stay at the short end (my judgment).

## 10. What reads as slop

39. **Feature laundry lists.** Explaining every feature is the most common mistake ([Motion the Agency](https://www.motiontheagency.com/blog/product-launch-video-guide)).
40. **Visible production.** Once viewers notice the animation instead of the product, you've lost them ([Okara](https://okara.ai/viral-launch-x-handbook)). The a16z founders say the same: cinematic filler adds nothing without substance ([Speedrun/a16z](https://speedrun.substack.com/p/how-to-make-a-viral-launch-video)).
41. **The logo-swap test.** If a competitor could paste in their logo and run your video, it's slop. Generic gradients, stock smiles and benefit lines that fit any product all fail it ([Blazon, "Stop the Slop"](https://blazonagency.com/post/stop-the-slop-product-launch)).
42. **The list of tells** (my judgment, consistent with the above): "Introducing...", a riser into a logo, whooshes on every cut, corporate ukulele or four-on-the-floor stock pop, 3D device spins, a full-window screenshot with a headline on top, a uniform-cadence TTS voice, emoji in captions, fake enthusiasm.
43. **Attention without trust.** Cluely's high-production ragebait film drew about 10M views and 70k signups ([The Colab Brief](https://www.thecolabbrief.com/p/cluely-s-ragebait-launch-went-viral-was-it-a-mess-or-masterpiece)), and analysts then argued it had won the wrong audience ([PostMVP](https://www.postmvp.com/p/cluely-nailed-their-launch-but-for)). Being provocative is fine. Being provocative about nothing true is not.

## 11. What reads as confident

44. **One idea.** The film makes a single claim and proves it once, clearly.
45. **Deadpan: the form plays it straight and the content is absurd.** The Onion's editors describe AP style as the straight man to the comedy, and they make sure everything except the joke is accurate ([Illinois Public Media, "How The Onion made modern news satire"](https://will.illinois.edu/transcript/news/best-of-how-the-onion-made-modern-news-satire)). The best SNL ad parodies copy the tone and format exactly, then land one punch line ([Boston Globe, best SNL commercial parodies](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2025/03/06/arts/best-snl-skits-commercials-ad-parodies-50-years/)).
46. **Specific details.** Real numbers, real UI and real names beat adjectives ([Blazon](https://blazonagency.com/post/stop-the-slop-product-launch)).
47. **Humor that trusts the audience.** The Browser Company's videos work because they're loose, playful and full of in-jokes, and they feel like a person made them ([Tella, "The Browser Company: Masters of Video"](https://www.tella.com/blog/the-browser-company-video); [How They Grow, "How Arc Grows"](https://www.howtheygrow.co/p/how-arc-grows)). Josh Miller says people can feel it when work comes from a real point of view rather than top-down ([Every podcast transcript](https://every.to/podcast/transcript-7d7adc44-103b-4483-a5a2-29a713085e33)). Never explain the joke.

## 12. Political-ad parody craft

48. **Copy the two registers of real campaign ads.** The *attack* register uses grainy black-and-white stills, a slow push-in and tense music ([NBC News, "This is your brain on negative ads"](https://www.nbcnews.com/id/wbna15549677), search result; [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/the-psychology-of-political-ads/)). The *morning* register uses warm, bright slow-motion montage, flags and swelling music, deliberately made to a higher standard than political ads usually get ([Retro Report](https://retroreport.org/transcript/transcript-morning-in-america-political-ads-that-changed-the-game/); [Living Room Candidate, "Prouder, Stronger, Better"](https://www.livingroomcandidate.org/commercials/1984/prouder-stronger-better)). Real ads often run both in one spot: attack the opponent, then offer the candidate.
49. **The narrator doesn't wink.** He's avuncular and ambiguous, and he points at something without shouting. "Bear in the woods" never names the threat ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bear_in_the_woods)).
50. **The disclaimer is the genre's signature, so get its details right.** US TV spots show the "paid for by" card for at least 4 s at no less than 4% of vertical picture height, with clear contrast. The candidate's approval line needs his image at 80% or more of frame height ([FEC, advertising and disclaimers](https://www.fec.gov/help-candidates-and-committees/advertising-and-disclaimers/)). Parodies get laughs by twisting the wording ([Encyclopedia of Satire, "Fake Campaign Ads"](https://satire.info/fake-campaign-ads/)). The same source advises labelling satire clearly.

## 12a. The "Apple framework" for expensive-looking product films

From leo (@leomeethewoo), "Make your product videos look expensive (Apple Framework)", X Article, 2026-09-25.
His team made 50+ product videos over the summer of 2026. His premise is that Apple's films feel premium
because every choice follows a system. What follows paraphrases a breakdown of the article made from a screen
recording of it; it is not a transcription.

51. **Intention and rules.** Nothing in the film is accidental: the font, the background, the music and the
    color theme each have a reason. Set real rules and keep them: a 3-color palette, the same backgrounds and
    one family of music for a type of video. The audience reads that consistency as a decision.
52. **Premium is mostly what you don't do.** The usual mistake is piling on effects, unnecessary sound effects
    and off-brand music.
53. **Design first.** Storyboard the film as finished frames before any animation (his team designs every
    frame in Figma first). A film won't look good without good design underneath.
54. **Framing.** One shot is one idea. Every scene gets room to breathe. The key object sits in the center of
    the frame. The background follows the brand and never competes with the key object.
55. **Smoothness.** Ease every keyframe's curve and overlap the keyframes of different elements. Linear
    motion looks cheap next to eased motion.
56. **Dynamic transitions.** Let one scene flow into the next (a carried element, a morph, a continuing move).
    Avoid direct cuts between scenes unless the message needs one.
57. **Adaptive rhythm.** Don't keep one pace: speed up, slow down or hold, depending on the part of the film.
58. **Pick the tempo first.** 60–80 BPM reads regal, cinematic, heritage. 90–110 reads smooth, cool,
    effortless. 115–123 reads elite, kinetic, sophisticated. Above that is drive and hype, which suits some
    projects and ruins others. Then choose the genre for the audience.
59. **Subtractive sound pass.** Sound design is the finishing touch; avoid effects that feel self-important.
    After placing the effects, watch the whole piece and remove any sound that's too loud, out of place, or
    doesn't help people understand the product.

For Netnyahoo this means: the palette is locked to the site's paper cream, ink black and campaign red, plus
the black-and-white attack look, with nothing off-palette. The campaign half's score sits at about 96 BPM
(smooth) and the attack half has no pulse. The black-and-white to color turn is the one deliberate hard cut;
every other change carries an element across or continues the camera's move. Proof shots are centered with
generous margins, the tally runs fast, and the proof shots and the "cleared" beat breathe.

## 13. Review checklist

Run every cut against this list. Anything answered "no" is a fix.

- [ ] Frame 0 is moving and already about the idea. No logo, no "Introducing".
- [ ] Hook text is on screen by 0.5 s, under 10 words, and the largest type in the piece.
- [ ] Muted on a phone at arm's length, the whole story and the joke still land.
- [ ] Caption lines are 6-8% of frame height, max 2 lines, on screen at least 1.5 s each, clear of the bottom edge.
- [ ] No shot holds longer than ~3.5 s. At least 12 shots in 25 s, with 2-3 deliberate holds.
- [ ] Major cuts land on downbeats, not on every beat. At least one deliberate break in the pattern.
- [ ] Every UI shot is a real recording, cropped so the key text is at least 3% of frame height. No full-window thumbnails.
- [ ] Cursor is enlarged, smoothed, and only on screen when it's doing something.
- [ ] Dull parts are ramped. The proof moment plays at 1x.
- [ ] No 3D device spin, no whoosh, no riser into the logo.
- [ ] Every SFX is tied to a visible action and lands on the frame where it completes.
- [ ] Music sounds performed. It's cut to length and ends on a resolution, not a fade.
- [ ] VO is 35-50 words, comped line by line, with hand-placed pauses and room tone under it. No pause over ~1 s unless it's the joke.
- [ ] Mix measures about -14 LUFS integrated with -1 dBTP peaks, checked on a phone speaker.
- [ ] Last frame leads into first. The loop seam goes unnoticed on the second pass.
- [ ] Logo-swap test fails for competitors: only Netnyahoo could have made this.
- [ ] Nothing explains the joke.

## 14. How it applies to Netnyahoo

1. **Structure it as a real two-part campaign spot** (my proposal). About 0-10 s is an attack ad on Chrome: grainy desaturated footage of real Chrome with ads and cookie banners, a slow push-in, low tense strings, a stamped word. About 10-22 s is the morning ad for Netnyahoo: color comes back, warm brass and strings, cropped real UI of the sidebar, the address bar and the uBlock counter. About 22-25 s is the end card "Impeach Chrome." plus a disclaimer that leads into frame 0.
2. **Make the disclaimer card the loop seam.** Use FEC-correct proportions (at least 4% of frame height, high contrast) and parody wording, for example "Paid for by Netnyahoo. Not approved by any court." Hold it about 2 s instead of the legal 4 s. Its styling can match the grainy first frame so the restart reads as part of the ad.
3. **Put the hook in the first frame, not the tagline.** Open on a real Chrome window at 1x with a burned-in attack line, for example "Chrome has been watching you." Save "Full immunity." for the turn at about 10 s, where the music changes.
4. **Every product claim gets one cropped proof shot:** the uBlock blocked-count ticking, an address bar entry in the sidebar, a profile opening as its own window, the Web Store "Add to Chrome" button working. Crop to that detail, hold 1.5-2.5 s, and ramp everything else. Never show the full window at 4:5.
5. **VO: 35-50 words, generic American campaign narrator, deadpan, never winking.** Don't clone or imitate Netanyahu's own voice. The narrator is the parody device; the public figure is the subject. Generate line by line, comp the takes, hand-place the pauses, and give the "Impeach Chrome." line a full beat of silence before it.
6. **Replace the synthetic score with a performed-sounding track.** Use a licensed library cue recorded by real players (strings and brass in the style of a campaign ad), or add real bowed and brass layers over the mock-up. Cut it so the key change lands on the "Full immunity" turn and the final chord resolves on the end card.
7. **SFX are foley only:** a real trackpad click on each cropped UI action, a paper-stamp thud on the attack stamp, and camera-shutter or flashbulb sounds on the grainy stills. No whooshes and no riser.
8. **Big Yahu appears once, in the morning section.** The mascot goes where a real ad puts the candidate: a slow-motion hero shot with a flag texture, so it reads as a campaign portrait and not a Pixar bumper (my judgment).
9. **Captions follow VO lines selectively** (rule 9). The joke lines get burned-in text. The rest of the screen belongs to campaign graphics: a lower third in slab serif, the stamp, the disclaimer.
10. **Run the checklist on a phone, muted, twice in a row.** The second viewing tests the loop. Then run it once with sound through the phone speaker at -14 LUFS.
